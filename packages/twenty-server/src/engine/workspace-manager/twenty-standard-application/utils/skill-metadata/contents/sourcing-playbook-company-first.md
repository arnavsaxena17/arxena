## Playbook: company list first

Use when the user wants a role across an industry ("HR head in pharmaceutical companies") or at a set of companies they did not name. Build the companies, then find the people.

0. **The brief, the sources and the scope come first.** One `ask_questions` batch before any paid call: which data sources to use (the `requirements` table, only the ones whose tool is in the catalog; pre-select the cheap set — the Arxena index when on, Bright Data **and Google search**, since Google is what finds people at small companies; offer Harvest too). Ask, do not infer the answer from what is configured. For a role plus an industry and country, also ask the scope the words leave open: **sub-segments** ("glass" spans float/architectural, container and packaging, glassware, automotive, fibre/scientific, processing/tempering), **size band**, **geography** (India-wide or a state/cluster; Indian HQ only or also Indian operations of foreign manufacturers), and **coverage** — all companies in the universe (exhaustive) or a top-N best-effort slice. A named industry in a named country is a universe: ask all vs top N even when the user did not say "all"; never default silently to ~100. For exhaustive, set `coverage: "exhaustive"`, `universe: {kind: "project", count}` and ask a run cap from the expected list size; after the list is confirmed, say if it does not cover pairs x per-pair (default $0.10). Record it with `get_sourcing_ledger` `brief`. Leave `target` null: this run's size is the company list. For sales, `ladder.maxRung` is 4 (owner/MD allowed at a small company) unless the user says otherwise; never set it to 2 to mean "exact title" — say the title reading in one line instead.
1. **Build the company list** with the `companies` section (Bright Data first). Score the companies against the user's company criteria before spending on people: industry, size, geography, and anything else that does not need a person.
1b. **One company fitment column before any people spend.** When the segment words can be met by a trader, distributor, services firm or an adjacent industry ("chemical manufacturer", "auto component maker", "retail chain"), score the list once: pass, fail, unknown. A Bright Data relevance flag is not that check. Keep pass, put unknown in review, and drop fail. Cheap, and it stops a plant-head search at a trading company.
2. **Confirm the exact list before any people spend**, every time, even after a yes on caps. Show the kept companies in a short table of **at most 10 names** (name, the criteria that decided it), the total count per segment and city, and one line on how many you dropped and why. Never read a long list of rows into the chat to show it: filter and count on the tab (`get_outreach_tab` with `filter`, `fields` and `groupBy`), show a 10-row sample, and quote the counts. Then `ask_questions` once: "Find {roles} at these {N} companies?" with **Yes, these {N}** (recommended), **Edit the list** and **Cancel**. On Edit, take the user's changes (names to add or drop, or a filter they applied on the Companies tab) and confirm again. Do not call `find_people_by_company` before a yes.
3. **Find the role at the approved companies only** with `find_people_by_company`, as `people-by-company` describes: the brief first (roles, persona, sources, ladder, coverage), a pilot of 10, the user's check, then the rest as a background run. Exhaustive does not stop at a target: it runs until every pair is terminal or the cap is spent. Pass the approved list as `companies` with row ids, or `fromProject: true, onlyViewMatches: true` when the Companies tab view shows exactly that list. Do not search company by company yourself.
4. **Score the people** by the funnel in the Vocabulary, using `scoring`. A person whose company fails the company criteria does not count. Anything the lookup could not settle — `criteriaUnknown`, or a criterion you did not pass as `criteria` — is one AI column on the People tab before the report; a row that fails a must-have is not in the count.
5. **Report**, coverage first: the coverage line from `get_sourcing_ledger` (`pairs.coverageLine`: company × role pairs covered, none found with reasons, needing review), then the found people per role, then the pairs without one and why, then companies searched and spend. A company with a CEO and no manufacturing head is one covered pair and one none_found pair, not "covered".

Company name plus designation on Google often yields names without LinkedIn URLs. `find_people_by_company` already resolves those URLs; do not do it by hand.
