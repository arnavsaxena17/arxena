## Playbook: company list first

Use when the user wants a role across an industry ("HR head in pharmaceutical companies") or at a set of companies they did not name. Build the companies, then find the people.

0. **One question batch, before any paid call.** Ask, at most four: which data
   sources; and, when the words leave it open, the sub-segment, the size band and
   **how many companies** — all, or the top N. A named industry in a country is a
   full set, so always offer both. Record the answers with `get_sourcing_ledger`
   `brief`. Do not ask for a row count here: the company list is the size of the
   run.
1. **Build the company list** with the `companies` section (Bright Data first). Score the companies against the user's company criteria before spending on people: industry, size, geography, and anything else that does not need a person.
1b. **One company fitment column before any people spend.** When the segment words can be met by a trader, distributor, services firm or an adjacent industry ("chemical manufacturer", "auto component maker", "retail chain"), score the list once: pass, fail, unknown. A Bright Data relevance flag is not that check. Keep pass, put unknown in review, and drop fail. Cheap, and it stops a plant-head search at a trading company.
2. **Bound the list to the number the user gave.** A stated count ("top 10", "top 25") counts **companies** unless the user said people. Sort the tab on the key that makes them "top" — usually `employees` — keep about N plus half again, and if the request named no key, say which you used. An unbounded industry list is the wrong answer to a bounded question: ten companies is a ten-company run, not a two-hundred-company one.
3. **Confirm the exact list before any people spend**, every time, even after a yes on caps. Show the kept companies in a short table of **at most 10 names** (name, the criteria that decided it), the total count per segment and city, and one line on how many you dropped and why. Filter and count on the tab (`get_outreach_tab` with `filter`, `sort`, `fields` and `groupBy`); show a 10-row sample; quote the counts. Then `ask_questions` once: "Find {roles} at these {N} companies?" with **Yes, these {N}** (recommended), **Edit the list** and **Cancel**. Do not call `find_people_by_company` before a yes.
4. **Find the role at the approved companies only** with `find_people_by_company`, as `people-by-company` describes: the brief first (roles, persona, sources, ladder, coverage), pass the approved list as `companies` with row ids — with `fromProject: true` only when the tab view shows exactly that list — then a pilot of 10, the user's check, then the rest. A pilot is the default above 25 companies; do not opt out with `skipPilot` unless the user says to run it all. Exhaustive does not stop at a target: it runs until every pair is terminal or the cap is spent. Do not search company by company yourself.
5. **Score the people** by the funnel in the Vocabulary, using `scoring`. A person whose company fails the company criteria does not count. Anything the lookup could not settle — `criteriaUnknown`, or a criterion you did not pass as `criteria` — is one AI column on the People tab before the report; a row that fails a must-have is not in the count.
6. **Report**, coverage first: the coverage line from `get_sourcing_ledger` (`pairs.coverageLine`: company × role pairs covered, none found with reasons, needing review), then the found people per role, then the pairs without one and why, then companies searched and spend. Read the run's `estimate` and `total` back against the plan line: a run over far more companies than the user asked for is a reason to stop before it spends, not after.

Company name plus designation on Google often yields names without LinkedIn URLs. `find_people_by_company` already resolves those URLs; do not do it by hand.
